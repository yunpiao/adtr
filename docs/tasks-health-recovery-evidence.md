## What the scenario proves

The owned fresh database contains precisely one submitted `infrastructure.health`/`platform` task and one recovery child. The initial task remains queued, attempt 0, while the owned controller has not started its worker. A real self-service password change advances this synthetic actor's authorization version and replaces its session. No grant, role, MFA reset, direct epoch edit or production account is involved.

Starting the production worker once causes its admission epoch check to reject the old task before the business attempt increments. Parent `failed`/`authorization_revoked`/attempt 0 therefore means authorization was revoked between admission and execution; it is not a health handler failure. Exact parent history is submitted → leased → authorization_rejected with attempts 0,0,0.

Recovery is explicit in the real task detail UI. Cancelling the first unopened intent must produce no POST and no stored task/event. Cancel clears that key. Reopening must yield a different submittedRecoveryKey. Only this reopened key may be submitted and later replayed. The child has current authority, the same payload and actor, and the original parent link. Exact child history is recovered → leased → started → progress → finished with attempts 0,0,1,1,1. Health returns database and queue ready. Parent immutable fields and ordered event/outbox identity payloads stay unchanged.

The explicit same-key replay is API-only, with a new current password proof and fresh TOTP, after confirmed successful recovery. It returns the same terminal child and appends no task/event/outbox. It is not a second UI confirmation. The cancelled key and original submission key are never substituted. Reload and normal history navigation must preserve the terminal records without another recovery POST.

## Health-only end-to-end job budget

The health job requires 30 minutes because its independent built_in_run provenance now requires real backend and frontend builds in addition to browser execution and owned cleanup. The earlier 20-minute total no longer covers that work conservatively. This change applies only to tasks-health-recovery; every existing suite retains its 20-minute job timeout. Its cost is up to ten additional runner-minutes for this new job, not extra proof attempts, worker restarts or looser acceptance.

Before the first checkout, the health workflow must record ADTR_E2E_HEALTH_JOB_DEADLINE as a positive-integer Unix epoch-seconds string set to the current wall time plus 1,620 seconds (27 minutes), through GITHUB_ENV. This early anchor makes checkout, setup, dependency preparation and builds consume the same budget rather than resetting it at runner launch. The deadline is mandatory for this suite: missing, malformed or already exhausted input fails closed. Do not silently create a fresh deadline in the runner or extend one after a timeout.

At runner entry, establish a 1,500-second monotonic ceiling and shorten it to the remaining inherited wall-clock deadline. Reserve the final 300 seconds of that effective runner window for owned cleanup. Business work, including real builds, setup and browser execution, must stop early enough to preserve that reserve; subprocess timeouts must be clamped to the remaining applicable aggregate deadline. Cleanup still attempts all independent required operations under its bounded remaining window, preserves the primary failure, and must not produce a successful receipt if any operation is skipped, incomplete or fails. A timeout never authorizes a second worker start or uncertain write retry.

The final 180 seconds of the 30-minute job are reserved outside the inherited 27-minute runner deadline for workflow-level joint validation and exact six-file upload. Each of those two steps is limited to at most 60 seconds, leaving the rest for workflow overhead. No receipt, final validation or upload may claim success merely because a timeout path was entered. Expiry during build, browser, cleanup, validation or upload is a real failed run.

The Playwright test ceiling remains 600 seconds, its browser subprocess cap remains 660 seconds, and all business-phase/TOTP ceilings above remain unchanged. These nested caps solve different problems: the test bounds the scenario, the subprocess bounds a stuck browser, the runner preserves cleanup time, and the job bounds the entire CI allocation. Removing the early deadline would permit setup/build time to escape the total budget; removing the cleanup reserve risks owned process/container leakage; increasing the outer job timeout alone fixes neither problem. These are prescribed ceilings, not measured runtimes. Actual CI must establish whether the genuine build and evidence path fits them.

For compatibility, the existing rules fixture field runnerMaxSeconds remains 660 and denotes only the browser-subprocess cap; it must not be used as the new overall runner deadline. No evidence-schema change is needed for this budget update. B must preserve ADTR_E2E_HEALTH_JOB_DEADLINE through runner environment sanitization, then enforce the monotonic/wall-clock intersection. The browser's existing Chromium environment stripping keeps this ADTR_-prefixed value out of Chromium alongside other Node-only controls.
